# SPK audience and language opportunities

Research date: 2026-10-06. Scope: initial organic adoption for SPK software,
primarily the local Kubernetes/Docker desktop application Ocular. About is a
supporting author/profile destination, not the primary product landing page.
Recommendations below are judgments, not measured conversion or donation rates.
No posts, messages, advertisements or telemetry have been created.

## What the numbers mean

GitHub account populations measure platform reach, not active professional
engineers, Kubernetes operators, desktop app buyers or prospective donors.
SlashData/CNCF cloud native developer estimates are closer to Ocular's audience,
but also include people who do not need its particular workflow. Do not add the
two populations together or treat survey percentages as national census results.
No comparable credible Iran-specific audience estimate was found in this research.

[GitHub Octoverse 2025](https://github.blog/news-insights/octoverse/octoverse-a-new-developer-joins-github-every-second-as-ai-leads-typescript-to-1/)
reports roughly 21.9 million accounts in India, 6.89 million in Brazil and
4.37 million in Indonesia. Those three communities more than quadrupled over
2020–2025; Japan and Germany more than tripled. These are dated GitHub figures,
not counts as of October 2026.

## Concrete channels, not promises of traffic

- [Show HN guidelines](https://news.ycombinator.com/showhn.html): submit work
  people can try and stay available to discuss it. This is a future candidate,
  not a prepared or published submission.
- [KCD Brasil](https://community2.cncf.io/kcd-brasil/) and
  [TabNews](https://www.tabnews.com.br/index): identifiable Portuguese-speaking
  technical communities. Recheck rules and current activity before posting.
- [V2EX example: Kite Desktop](https://us.v2ex.com/t/1207069): a maintainer
  introduced an open-source Wails-based Kubernetes desktop tool in 2026. This
  is direct evidence of relevant conversation and competition, not proof that
  Ocular will acquire users. Do not copy the competitor's claims.
- [Qiita](https://qiita.com/about) and [Zenn](https://zenn.dev/about): Japanese
  technical knowledge communities; lead with an instructive workflow.
- [Virgool Kubernetes tag](https://virgool.io/tag/kubernetes): current Persian
  technical discussion. Presence does not establish audience size or permission
  for promotional posts.
- [CNCF community groups](https://github.com/cncf/communitygroups): find a
  relevant active group and follow its own participation rules.

## Regional access and adoption limits

Before a China or Iran pilot, independently verify the complete path from the
local network: website, GitHub API, release asset, checksum, installer and first
connection. This study has not tested those networks; do not assert nationwide
availability or unavailability. Avoid mandatory external fonts, analytics or
accounts. Do not create unverified third-party binary mirrors.

[GitHub's current trade-controls policy](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/other-site-policies/github-and-trade-controls)
says its license generally covers public/private, free/paid services in Iran,
subject to restricted-party exceptions. This is not proof of reliable local
connectivity, availability of every adjacent service, or payment access.

For every region, confirm actual OS/version support and installation friction.
A Linux glibc 2.39 requirement, unsigned Windows installers and non-notarized
macOS builds can outweigh the benefit of translated marketing. This observation
does not authorize changing application packaging or support policies.

Do not equate adoption potential with donations. No credible regional donation
conversion data for SPK was found, and cryptocurrency-only support is an
additional action some users will not take. Keep product usefulness first.

## Proposed sequence and evaluation

1. English baseline and Russian existing audience; collect actionable feedback
   on one complete workflow.
2. Focused English/India and Portuguese/Brazil pilots; prepare Chinese and
   Japanese pages with installation guidance.
3. Chinese and Japanese pilots after local-script review and download testing.
4. Persian/Iran small feedback cohort; German/Spanish and Indonesian experiments
   according to actual responses and maintenance capacity.

Candidate website coverage: the launcher's eight application languages
(English, Russian, simplified Chinese, Spanish, German, French, Portuguese,
Japanese), with Persian deferred until terminology and language review are adequate.
The initial website implementation uses these eight languages. This does not
imply the Ocular application or all its documentation has eight-language coverage.
The owner explicitly prioritizes natural, accurate translations over language count.

For a small pilot, ask volunteers whether they installed, connected, completed
one task and encountered a language/installation blocker. Track these voluntary
reports without introducing analytics. A handful of successful attempts is
qualitative evidence, not statistical proof of market size. Stars and page views
alone are inadequate. Outreach starts only under a separate explicit request.

## Translation quality evidence and selection limits

The owner's condition is to avoid languages with known poor translations, rather
than maximizing the language count. Research does not justify a blanket statement
that Persian is untranslatable by AI:

- [Heierli et al., 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.lowresnlp-1.12/) reports
  linguistic and cultural weaknesses across four evaluated models in Persian.
  Its task suite is broader than technical website translation, so cultural
  shortcomings cannot automatically be equated with failure on installation copy.
- [Rezaeimanesh et al., 2025 revision](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09993)
  reports strong results for one model on Persian/English idiom translation and
  differences by translation direction and prompting. An idiom benchmark also
  does not certify a software website.
- [WMT25 findings](https://aclanthology.org/2025.wmt-1.22/) evaluated systems
  across language pairs and domains with professional annotators. This supports
  evaluating actual translated content in context rather than relying solely on
  one aggregate automated score.

These studies concern their named 2024–2025 systems, not an evaluation of this
session's model or a native-speaker review of the finished websites. Persian is
therefore deferred for the initial scope because terminology review and RTL QA
are not yet established; the research is not a permanent language blacklist.
For every published locale, known unresolved meaning errors block release.
Technical invariants and browser checks supplement linguistic review rather than
substitute for it. Independent native-speaker certification has not been performed.
